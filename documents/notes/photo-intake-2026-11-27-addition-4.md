## Cross-collection connections

- **Aokigahara:** photo-174 explicitly names Fugaku Wind Cave and its natural-refrigerator display. [Yamanashi Official Travel Guide](https://www.yamanashi-kankou.jp/english/recover/fugaku-wind-cave.html) places it in Aokigahara and describes its historic use as a natural refrigerator. Connect it to the 24 July cave visit, without assuming it identifies the second cave.
- **Saiko:** photo-177 names Saiko East Gate. It complements the PICA Fuji Saiko / Lake Saiko cluster, but does not establish a specific cycling or walking trail.
- **Arrival:** photo-178 is a physical ticket record for the Haneda–Shinjuku journey on 21 July 2026.
- **Tagonoura / Fuji City:** photos-185–186 name the port and its management office. They belong in the broader region of existing Fujisan Dragon Tower / Tagonoura Minato Park photos photo-147–148, but the port office and park remain separate. [Shizuoka Prefecture — Tagonoura Port Management Office](https://www.pref.shizuoka.jp/kensei/shisetsu/doboku/1008978.html).
- **Lake Kawaguchi:** photos-187–188 form a Tenjoyama ropeway sequence. [Yamanashi official tourism](https://www.yamanashi-kankou.jp/fc/location/17/17009.html) identifies the Mt. Fuji Panoramic Ropeway and lake-view stations.
- **Nagahama shop:** photo-183 identifies Kadoya Shoten; the [Fuji Five Lakes tourism listing](https://fujisan.ne.jp/sightseeing/1860/) gives Nagahama 1338-7. DYNAKYRIS describes a shop/residence but does not name it, so the match is not asserted.
- **Fireworks candidate:** photos-179–180 have no unique landmark. DYNAKYRIS's 16 August entry describes a local celebration the previous evening. Fujikawaguchiko Town says Nagahama holds its Furusato Festival each 15 August ([town page](https://www.town.fujikawaguchiko.lg.jp/ka/info.php?if_id=740)); the [2026 event listing](https://www.walkerplus.com/event/ar0419e195711/data.html) gives fireworks at Nagahama Shinsui Park on 15 August. This is plausible but unconfirmed; no festival map pin is added.
- **Kyoto / Osaka:** photos-196–197 identify Osaka Castle, distinct from Odawara and Iga-Ueno. Photo-201 identifies CRYSTA Nagahori; its [official access page](https://global.crystaweb.jp/en/access/) says it is connected to Shinsaibashi and Nagahoribashi stations.
- **Jihanki:** photo-193 adds an alcohol vending-machine subject, not a known location. Photos-181–182 are a paired R-7 emergency-phone installation. Other tunnels and underground passages remain separate until identified.

The remaining open questions are the exact Tokyo-area skyline location (photo-176), the highway tunnel (photo-189), the precise arcade venue (photo-195), the exact buildings/street near Shinsaibashi (photo-199–200), and the school name beside Miyuki Beach (photo-202).
