## Identification confidence

The audit uses four distinct levels. They should not be collapsed into a single statement of certainty.

1. **Named / source-confirmed:** a place or object is readable in the photograph, or the image is directly matched to a dated expedition note.
2. **Strong landmark match:** an identifiable landmark and the trip chronology point to a named place, even if no street address is visible.
3. **Regional or sequence match:** the image belongs to a known part of the journey, but the specific viewpoint, street, building or venue is unknown.
4. **Theme only / unresolved:** the photograph is useful to the archive as a visual motif, but there is not enough evidence for a geographic or event attribution.

The map remains schematic. No GPS coordinates, exact road traces, private accommodation details or venue names are invented.

### Arakurayama Sengen Park — Chureito Pagoda and the 26 July night view

**Records:** `photo-13`, `photo-46`, `photo-85`, `photo-101`, `photo-114`, `photo-116`

The five-storey pagoda photographs and the Fuji / near-full-Moon / town-light views form one coherent group. ## Cross-collection visual themes worth preserving

### Night infrastructure as a presence

The forest telephone booth, vending machines, tunnel exit marker, pedestrian signals, road mirrors, bus terminal, and isolated streetlights form a visual vocabulary of infrastructure at the edge of human activity. This reinforces the Jihanki concept already present in the sound and video projects: mundane machines and road objects gain a different character when encountered at night, in rain, or in an otherwise empty landscape.

### Water as a recurring boundary

Several different water scenes should remain distinct in the archive: storm coast and harbour beacon (`photo-91`–`photo-92`), night lake and reflected beam (`photo-97`, `photo-109`, `photo-120`, `photo-125`), stream and bridge (`photo-117`–`photo-118`), industrial waterway and steam (`photo-90`, `photo-129`), and the Kyoto river (`photo-139`). Their similarity is thematic, not proof that they share a place.

### Records, toys, games and physical media

The retro museum photographs and the separate arcade image give the journey a second layer of machine history: not only contemporary vending infrastructure, but also obsolete games, toys, monster figures, records and the material culture of listening. This sits naturally beside the July 28 attempt to leave CDs and cassettes with independent music shops, but the photographs alone do not prove that the record display was part of a music-shop visit.

### Framed landscapes and ordinary rituals

The room in `photo-137` shows the paired circular and square garden windows associated with Genko-an. This is now a strong visual match to the traveller-confirmed Kyoto visit and is linked to the additional Genko-an interior images (`photo-151`–`photo-153`). The earlier Fuji image (`photo-14`) is a separate rectangular multi-pane wooden lattice view. The flower still lifes (`photo-96`, `photo-112`), roadside stone marker (`photo-106`), lanterns and summit torii extend the project’s interest in small ritual objects and the act of framing a landscape. These are editorial groupings, not identifications of a particular shrine or botanical species.
