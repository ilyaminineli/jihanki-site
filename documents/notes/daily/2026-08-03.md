## Museum of Military Aircraft

We visited a museum dedicated to military aircraft.

The atmosphere was distinctly Japanese: a mixture of technology, history, preservation and memory.

Eli bought a book there.

The visit reinforced the idea that Japan's relationship with technology is not only futuristic and digital. It also encompasses preserved machinery, historical memory and artefacts carrying stories from previous generations.

## Koba-san drives us to Satyam 1 — the former Kamikuishiki compound

Koba-san drove us to an interesting place in the former Kamikuishiki area. I was staying with him, and it was about a half-hour drive from where Takismana and I were living around Saiko.

There was a memorial and a small open field where the former Aum Shinrikyo compound had stood. The buildings had been removed, leaving a very different landscape. We understood the site as part of the organisation's major Kamikuishiki centre and as a place connected with the history of its facilities under Shoko Asahara.

### Historical correction — distinguish Satyam 1 from Satyam No. 7

The wider former compound was not a single factory. Japan's Public Security Intelligence Agency describes a large Aum facility complex at Fujigamine in the former Kamikuishiki Village, covering roughly 45,100 square metres and containing several groups of facilities called Satyans. The Tokyo Photographic Art Museum catalogues a 1995 photograph using the title **“Aum Shinrikyo Satyam 1, Kamikuishiki-mura (now Fujikawaguchiko-machi), Yamanashi Prefecture.”** That supports the archive's Satyam 1 place-name, but it does not make Satyam 1 the sarin plant.

The Ministry of Justice's location archive identifies **Satyam No. 7 (第7サティアン)** in the Kamikuishiki compound as the chemical plant intended for sarin production. Its incident summary describes the plant as nearly completed and put into operation as part of an attempt to produce sarin on a very large scale. An academic history of the site, however, describes the chemical plant as never completed. For that reason this archive calls No. 7 the *planned / near-completed sarin plant* and does not claim that the proposed industrial output was successfully manufactured. It is kept distinct from the traveller-identified Satyam 1 location.

Aum's earlier sarin history also needs a little precision. The Ministry of Justice chronology records sarin-spraying incidents near associated facilities in late 1993, the Matsumoto sarin attack in June 1994, and the Tokyo subway attack on 20 March 1995. These events belong to the wider history of the organisation; they should not be collapsed into a claim that the Satyam 1 building itself was where all the sarin was produced.

Public historical accounts describe the former compound buildings being demolished and the land later becoming a public park. An academic history notes that a memorial was erected there, but that the stone itself only says **“cenotaph” (慰霊碑)** and does not explain its purpose. The traveller's memory of a memorial at the site is retained; the archive does not assert that the stone's inscription specifically identifies the Tokyo subway victims.

## The localised cloud above Mount Fuji

One of the most striking things we noticed was a large cloud or mist rising around Mount Fuji. It looked like fog confined to a particular part of the landscape, almost like a localised plume. It was difficult to capture in a photograph.

This remains a visual observation and a memory of the weather and landscape that day. The archive does not imply that the cloud was connected to the former Aum facilities, their chemistry or any industrial residue.

## Possible themes

- Invisible histories beneath ordinary landscapes.
- The mountain as a witness.
- Human actions that leave traces after buildings disappear.
- A place that changes from an active compound into a quiet field and memorial landscape.

## Project note

The expedition began by searching for yōkai as folklore. By the end, the definition had expanded. A yōkai could also be a place whose history is difficult to see after its buildings have gone.
